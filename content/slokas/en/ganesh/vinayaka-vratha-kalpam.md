# Vinayaka Vratha Kalpam

**Pooja Samagri**

Turmeric, kumkum, sandal paste, akshata, flowers, fruit, betel leaves, betel
nuts, coconut, incense, lamp and its oil, panchamrita, undrallu, kudumulu and
modakam, the twenty-one kinds of leaves, durva grass, and a clay Ganesha idol.

## Sankalpam

Mama Upatta Duritakshayadvara Shri Parameshvara Prityartham Shubhe Shobhane
Muhurte, Adya Brahmanah Dvitiya Pararthe Shvetavaraha Kalpe Vaivasvata
Manvantare Kaliyuge Prathama Pade Jambudvipe Bharatavarshe Bharatakhande
Meroh Dakshina Digbhage, Asmin Vartamana Vyavaharika Chandramanena
Shri ... Nama Samvatsare Dakshinayane Varsha Ritau Bhadrapada Mase
Shukla Pakshe Chaturthyam Tithau ... Vasare Shubha Nakshatre Shubha Yoge
Shubha Karana Evamguna Visheshana Vishishtayam Shubha Tithau Mama Kshema
Sthairya Dhairya Vijaya Ayurarogya Aishvaryabhivriddhyartham,
Dharmartha Kama Moksha Chaturvidha Phala Purushartha Siddhyartham,
Siddhi Buddhi Sameta Shri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Devata Muddishya
Shri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Devata Prityartham
Kalpokta Prakarena Yavachchhakti Dhyanavahanadi Shodashopachara Poojam
Karishye ॥

## Vratha Katha

In an earlier age the elephant-faced Gajasura performed severe penance to
Shiva and won the boon that Shiva dwell within his belly. With Shiva so
confined, Parvati could not bear the separation and prayed to Vishnu. Vishnu
came in the guise of a keeper of performing bulls, made Nandi dance, pleased
Gajasura and had Shiva released.

<br>

Awaiting Shiva's return, Parvati formed a boy from the turmeric paste of her
bath, gave him life, and set him to guard her door. When the boy would not let
him in, Shiva severed his head. Seeing Parvati's grief, they brought the head
of Gajasura, fixed it upon the boy and revived him. That elephant-faced one is
Vinayaka, and he was given lordship over the ganas and over all obstacles.

<br>

On the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada the gods worshipped
Vinayaka and offered him kudumulu. Having eaten his fill, Vinayaka was riding
his mouse when it started at the sight of a serpent, and he fell. His belly
split and the offerings scattered; he gathered them back, filled his belly
again and bound that same serpent about his waist. Seeing this, the moon
laughed from the sky.

<br>

The angered Vinayaka cursed the moon: "Whoever looks upon you shall bear false
blame." When the curse brought distress upon the world, at the gods' prayer he
softened it — that the moon must not be looked upon on that one day, Bhadrapada
Shukla Chaturthi; and that one who does so by mistake will be freed of the
blame by hearing this vratha katha and placing akshata upon the head.

<br>

Later Shri Krishna looked upon the moon on that day unknowingly, and bore the
false charge of stealing the Syamantaka gem. He fought Jambavan, recovered the
gem, and on Narada's counsel performed the Vinayaka Vratham and was freed of
the blame. It is for this reason that the vratham is kept, the katha heard,
and akshata placed upon the head.

<br>

## Mangala Harathi

Om Shri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Svamine Namah ।  
Karpura Nirajanam Samarpayami ॥

Jaya Mangalam Nitya Shubha Mangalam  
Siddhi Vinayaka Svamiki Mangalam ॥

## Udvasana

Yajnena Yajnamayajanta Devah Tani Dharmani Prathamanyasan  
Te Ha Nakam Mahimanah Sachante Yatra Purve Sadhyah Santi Devah ॥

Shri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Svamine Namah । Yathasthanam Udvasayami ॥

Anaya Dhyanavahanadi Shodashopachara Poojaya Bhagavan Sarvatmakah
Shri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Devata Suprita Suprasanno Varado Bhavatu ॥
